You have the question.
litscout finds the papers.

Describe what you want to find out and what you work on. litscout searches the scholarly literature, reads the papers that matter, writes an outlook for your work, and then answers your questions with the page each answer came from.

Download litscout How to set it up

Free and open source (MIT). Runs in the Claude desktop app on Mac and Windows. You only chat.

Three steps. No coding.

1

Download and unzip

Click Download litscout above, unzip it, and move the litscout folder to your Documents.

2

Open it in Claude

In the Claude desktop app, go to the Code tab and open the litscout folder.

3

Type “start”

Claude sets itself up, asks your research question and a few lines about your work, shows you the reading list, reads the papers, and writes your outlook.

You need the Claude desktop app (its Code tab). A paid plan (Pro or higher) is strongly recommended: on the free plan the limit usually runs out partway; your progress is saved, and you type continue when it resets. No programming, no terminal, no API key. Programmers: see the README.

What you get

reading_list.md

The papers worth reading, each with a relevance score and one line on why. You can add or drop papers before anything is read.

cards/

One note per paper: findings with page numbers, methods, materials, key numbers, limits, and how it bears on your work.

outlook.md

Bottom line, a map of the themes, where papers agree and conflict, gaps, opportunities for your own work, and a reading order.

ask

Type any question about your papers. Answers cite the paper and page, and say so when the papers do not cover it.

Where the papers come from

  • OpenAlex, an open index of over 250 million scholarly works, and arXiv.
  • It also follows the references and citations of the best matches.
  • Open-access PDFs download by themselves. For the rest it gives you a list of links: save them from your library into the project folder and it reads them too.

What it will never do

  • Make up a paper, a number or a page number on purpose: every claim is tied to a paper and page you can check.
  • Get around a publisher's download block or paywall.
  • Pretend a paper was read in full when only its abstract was available; the outlook says which ones.

What it costs

litscout is free. You pay for your Claude plan.

Questions

Do I need to know how to code?

No. You talk to Claude in plain English. Claude runs everything, installs what it needs the first time, and tells you if it ever needs a click from you.

What can I say to it?

“start”, your research question, “continue”, “find more papers on …”, “go deeper”, “read the Smith paper in full”, “show my reading list”, “drop the third paper”, “I added PDFs”, “update the outlook”, “where am I?”, or any question about your papers.

Can I add papers I already have?

Yes. Download them through your library as usual and tell Claude “I added PDFs”; it files and reads them. Your own reports and papers can be added the same way.

Where are my files kept?

On your computer, in the My Research folder inside litscout, one folder per question. Open litscout in Claude again any day and keep asking.

Is my data private?

The files stay on your computer, but Claude reads the text of the papers and your questions. Do not use it on documents you are not allowed to share.

I ran out of Claude usage. Did I lose my work?

No. Every step is saved in your project folder. When your limit resets, open the litscout folder in Claude again and type “continue”. To use less, ask for fewer papers (10 instead of 15).

Does it read figures?

No. It reads the text of each paper. Scanned PDFs without a text layer are used from their abstract only.

Can I trust the outlook?

Treat it as a well-organised first draft. Skim the reading list before it reads anything, and check the cited pages before you quote an answer.
